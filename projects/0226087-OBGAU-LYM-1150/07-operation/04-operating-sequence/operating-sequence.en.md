# 7.4 Washing cycle and part loading

This section describes a complete washing cycle, from loading the parts into the basket to unloading the washed parts. Before the cycle is started, the machine must have been started up in accordance with **Chapter 7.2** and the process water must have reached the target temperature.

## 7.4.1 Loading the parts

The parts are loaded directly into the rotating basket with the lid open. Small parts can be washed in wire parts baskets placed on the basket. On machines with the transport trolley option, the basket can be pulled out onto the trolley, loaded outside the machine and pushed back into the machine loaded (**See Chapter 5.1.2**).

![Placing the wire parts basket on the rotating basket](../../assets/7.4/sepet-yukleme.jpg)

*Figure 7.1 — Placing the wire parts basket on the rotating basket*

1. Release the lid latch and open the lid fully; check that the gas springs hold the lid in the open position. If the lid does not stay in the open position, stop loading and have the gas springs replaced (**See Chapter 9.1.7**).
2. If necessary, rotate the basket to a suitable position for loading using the TEST button; then release the button and check that the basket has stopped.
3. Place the parts in the basket so that the weight is evenly distributed over the basket. Position heavy parts close to the centre of the basket.
4. Place parts with blind holes and internal cavities with the hole openings facing downwards or sideways, so that the washing water drains out without collecting in the cavities.
5. Put small parts into the wire parts basket placed on the basket; prevent them from being thrown about during spraying.
6. Check that the parts do not protrude beyond the edges of the basket and will not touch the spray arms when the lid is closed.
7. Check that the total load does not exceed the maximum loading capacity and the maximum loading height (**See Chapter 3.3.2**).

**WARNING — Heavy load:** Lifting heavy parts by hand can cause back injuries, and a falling part can cause foot injuries. Load parts that exceed the manual handling limits of the operating company using lifting equipment or the transport trolley. When loading with lifting equipment, lower the load slowly onto the basket; do not drop it onto the basket with impact.

## 7.4.2 Starting the washing cycle

1. Close the lid and engage the lid latch.
2. Check that the washing time on the timer is at the value specified for the part type (**See Chapter 6.3.2**).
3. Check that the measured temperature (PV) on the thermostat display has reached the target temperature.
4. Press the green START button.
5. Check that the timer has started counting down.

During the cycle, the basket rotates and the washing pump sprays the process water onto the parts through the nozzles; on machines with the interlock lock option, the lid remains locked throughout the cycle. The sprayed water returns to the tank, passes through the filters and is drawn into the pump again. No operator intervention is required during the cycle.

## 7.4.3 End of cycle and unloading

1. When the timer reaches zero, check that the pump and the basket have stopped.
2. Wait a short time for the steam in the chamber to disperse. The steam is released through the steam vent outlets on the side surfaces; on machines with a steam extraction fan, the fan drives the steam into the extraction duct.
3. Release the lid latch and open the lid; keep your face away from the lid opening.
4. Wait for the water on the parts to drain into the basket. On machines with a drying fan, carry out the drying step.
5. Remove the parts from the basket wearing heat-resistant protective gloves, or pull the basket out onto the transport trolley.

**WARNING — Hot parts:** After washing, the parts and the basket are at a temperature close to that of the process water and can cause burns. Do not touch the parts without heat-resistant gloves.

## 7.4.4 Machine behaviour in the event of a fault

| Condition | Machine behaviour | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Emergency stop button pressed | All functions stop | **See Chapter 2.5** |
| Low water level | Heater and pump are disabled; safety circuit opens | **See Chapter 11**, Fault 3 |
| Gear motor thermal overload relay tripped | Basket stops | **See Chapter 11**, Fault 1 |
| Pump thermal overload relay tripped | Pump stops | **See Chapter 11**, Fault 2 |
| Optional equipment (drying fan, oil skimmer, etc.) not working | The equipment concerned stops; no fault lamp lights up | **See Chapter 11**, Fault 4 |
| Power failure | Machine stops; does not restart automatically when power returns | Press the RESET button and restart the cycle |
