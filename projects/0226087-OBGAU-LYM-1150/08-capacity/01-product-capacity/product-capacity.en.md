# 8.1 Capacity limits and determining the capacity

The hourly capacity of the machine is determined by the size, weight and geometry of the part, the type and degree of contamination, the required cleanliness level and the resulting washing time. For this reason the manufacturer does not state a nominal capacity value in parts/hour or kg/hour.

## 8.1.1 Capacity limits

The following physical limits must not be exceeded in capacity planning. The limit values are given in **Chapter 3.3**.

| Limit | Value | Reference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maximum weight that can be loaded per cycle** | 400 kg | **Chapter 3.3.2** |
| **Maximum loading height** | 550 mm | **Chapter 3.3.2** |
| **Basket diameter** | 1000 mm | **Chapter 3.3.2** |
| **Washing time** | 0–100 min | **Chapter 3.3.3** |
| **Continuous operation** | 24/7 | **Chapter 3.3.3** |

## 8.1.2 Calculating the capacity

The hourly capacity for a given part type is calculated as follows:

1. Determine the number of parts that can be placed in the basket per cycle without exceeding the weight and height limits and leaving enough space between the parts for the spray water to reach them.
2. Determine the washing time required for the part type (**See Chapter 6.7**).
3. Measure the time required for loading, unloading and opening and closing the lid.
4. Calculate the total duration of one cycle (washing time + loading and unloading time).
5. Calculate the hourly capacity by dividing 60 minutes by the total cycle time and multiplying the result by the number of parts per cycle.

On machines with a transport trolley, preparing a second basket outside the machine while one basket is being washed shortens the loading time and increases the capacity. On machines without a transport trolley, the loading time can be shortened by arranging the parts for the next cycle in wire parts baskets in advance.

Packing the parts tightly into the basket or stacking them on top of one another increases the number of parts per cycle, but prevents the spray water from reaching the part surfaces and lowers the washing quality. In that case the required washing time becomes longer and no increase in capacity is achieved.
