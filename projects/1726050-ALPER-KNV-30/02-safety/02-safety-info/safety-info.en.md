# 2.2 Residual risks and machine labels

An EN ISO 12100 risk assessment was applied at the design stage; risks that could be reduced by design have been reduced. Nevertheless **residual risks** remain that cannot be eliminated completely due to the nature of the machine. Personnel must be aware of these risks; must use the related PPE and must follow the **Chapter 2.6** matrix.

Warning labels on the machine are explained with general symbol rules in **Chapter 1.2**; this chapter defines machine-specific residual risks and label locations.

---

## 2.2.1 Residual risks

**Thermal risk — hot process fluid and surfaces**

Process fluid in the wash and rinse tanks is heated; water inlet temperature may be in the range **+10°C to +70°C** (**See Chapter 3.3.5**). Tank, pipe, heater and part surfaces can remain hot after the process as well. Contact with bare hands creates the risk of first- or second-degree burns. Allow sufficient time for the system to cool before maintenance or cleaning.

**Mechanical risk — conveyor and moving parts**

The conveyor moves continuously or on a start command; there is a trapping and crushing risk in the part infeed/outfeed zone. While the machine is running, do not extend hand, foot or tool into the conveyor line, pump/fan housings or moving mechanism. Even on a robot-integrated line, emergency-stop and RFID functions remain in service.

**Chemical and liquid exposure**

Wash and rinse processes contain oil, dirt and process fluid. During filter cleaning, tank maintenance and leakage there is a splash risk to skin or eyes. Water detection in the drip tray (HMI alarm Error-452) creates a slippery floor and slipping risk.

**Electrical risk**

The electrical cabinet contains **380 V**, **50 Hz**, **3-phase** supply (**See Chapter 3.3.3**). Only authorised electrical personnel may open the cabinet door; the **Chapter 2.4** LOTO procedure is mandatory before intervention. Even if the main switch is off, hazardous voltage can remain for a short time in drive/capacitor circuits.

**Pneumatic risk**

Compressed-air supply is **6 bar** (**See Chapter 3.3.5**). Injury risk can arise when line pressure is discharged suddenly or a connection breaks. Isolate pressure during pneumatic interventions and vent residual pressure (**See Chapter 2.4**).

**Noise**

Machine noise level is defined as **65 dB(A)** (**See Chapter 3.3.6**). During long-duration close-range work, additional hearing protection assessment may be required according to the employer’s workplace noise legislation.

---

## 2.2.2 Warning labels on the machine

Pictograms on the machine body and electrical cabinet are compatible with ISO 7010. Removing, painting over or making labels unreadable is prohibited.

| Label / symbol | Typical location | Required action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| General hazard | Machine body, process zones | **See Chapter 2** |
| Hot surface | Near tank and heater | Do not touch until cooled; heat-resistant gloves |
| Corrosive / chemical | Near tank and dosing | Use PPE; do not make direct contact |
| Crushing / trapping | Conveyor infeed/outfeed | Do not enter the zone while running |
| Slippery floor | Machine base, drip tray | Remove the leak; walk carefully |
| Electrical hazard | Electrical cabinet | Authorised personnel only; apply LOTO |
| PPE symbols | Operator and maintenance zones | **See Chapter 2.6** |

Renew worn labels immediately (**See Chapter 1.3**). Do not operate a machine without labels.
